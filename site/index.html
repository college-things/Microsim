Draw a circuit. Run it. Watch it work.

Microsim is a lightweight circuit simulator. Place parts, wire them, and read the result on oscilloscopes, meters and plotters while the circuit runs. It simulates analog and digital parts and 8051 microcontrollers, and it opens Multisim .ms14 circuits.

Download Open in your browser

Free and open source under the MIT license. Version 0.9 beta.

What it does

A real circuit simulator

A SPICE-style engine runs operating point, DC sweep, AC, transient, noise, pole-zero, transfer function and sensitivity analyses. Parameter, temperature and nested sweeps, Monte Carlo, worst case, distortion and noise figure come on top.

Instruments that run live

Oscilloscopes, multimeters, a function generator, Bode plotter, wattmeter, logic analyzer and serial terminal update as the circuit runs. A frequency counter, spectrum analyzer, distortion analyzer, word generator, logic converter and IV curve tracer are also included.

Opens Multisim files

Open .ms14 circuits, including NI's own samples, and save back to .ms14. Parts from an installed NI database can be imported.

Microcontrollers

An 8051 runs your program in the circuit, with its timers, interrupts and serial port, a 16x2 LCD and a keypad. Assemble your source in the app or load Intel HEX, then set breakpoints and step through it in the debugger.

Parts, reports and boards

Common transistors, diodes, op-amps, the 555, regulators and 74HC logic, with original models. Print to PDF with a title block, list a bill of materials, check electrical rules, and lay out a PCB with Gerber and KiCad export.

An assistant that knows the circuit

Ask the built-in assistant to explain a circuit, find a fault or build something. It uses your own Anthropic API key, which stays on your device.

What it isn't

Microsim is an independent program, not a copy of Multisim. It covers what most students and hobbyists use day to day, but not everything:

  • Its own part library is small. Parts from an installed NI database can be imported, but NI's collection is not included.
  • PIC microcontrollers don't run yet, and LCD and keypad parts in Multisim circuits aren't converted (Microsim's own LCD and keypad work).
  • There is no Ultiboard compatibility, and LabVIEW .vi files only run through an installed LabVIEW.
  • Its results have not been systematically compared with Multisim's.

The full, current list is in docs/limits.md.
